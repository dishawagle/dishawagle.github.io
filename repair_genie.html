Stage 01 · Problem statement

The repair gap

"Most homeowners facing a broken appliance have no reliable way to understand what's wrong or who to trust with the repair."

Problem statement · derived from product thinking · June 2026

The homeowner experience is broken

When an appliance fails, a homeowner faces a cascade of uncertainty: What's actually wrong? Is it safe to touch? Can I fix it myself? How much should I pay? Who should I call? The current options are all suboptimal.

Search online

Generic advice that may not match their specific model or symptom.

Call a contractor cold

No idea if the quote is fair or the problem is correctly diagnosed.

Replace the appliance

Out of frustration — often for something fixable for $30.

The contractor lead problem

Local appliance repair contractors face the opposite problem: low-quality, unqualified leads. A typical enquiry arrives with no diagnostic context, no confirmation the homeowner is serious, and no filter for whether the contractor services that appliance type or area. The result is high call volume, low conversion, and significant time wasted on diagnostic conversations that should have happened before the first call.

What contractors need

Qualified leads for appliances they actually fix, in areas they actually serve, from homeowners who are ready to hire.

The gap Repair Genie fills

No product simultaneously gives homeowners an AI-powered diagnosis and routes context-rich leads to relevant contractors. That's the gap.

Stage 02 · Users & personas

Two sides, three archetypes

Homeowner — the DIY-curious fixer

Who they areHomeowner, 28–55, may have attempted DIY before. Comfortable with smartphones but not technically trained on appliances.
TriggerAppliance broke this morning. Need it working, ideally without spending $300.
Jobs to be doneUnderstand what's wrong. Know if it's safe to touch. Try to fix it themselves. If they can't, find someone trustworthy without getting ripped off.
FrustrationsGeneric YouTube tutorials that don't match their model. Contractors who quote high without diagnosing. Not knowing if a repair is worth it vs. replacing.
SuccessFixed the dryer for $15 and 45 minutes, or found a trustworthy contractor who knew exactly what was wrong before arriving.

Homeowner — needs a pro

A subset of homeowners will skip or fail the DIY path. They still want the AI diagnosis — both for peace of mind and to feel confident they're not being overcharged — but their primary goal is a qualified contractor who can come today.

Contractor — the local repair professional

Who they areIndependent repair technician or small repair business. Services 1–3 appliance categories. Operates within a defined geographic radius.
TriggerWants more jobs without spending money on ads or answering unqualified calls.
Jobs to be doneReceive job requests for appliances they actually fix, in areas they actually serve, from homeowners who are ready to hire.
FrustrationsLeads who haven't decided to hire. Jobs outside service area. Arriving with no context. Time wasted on diagnostic calls.
SuccessA job request arrives with clear problem description. Contractor accepts, arrives, and resolves within one visit.
Stage 03 · Solution & flows

A two-sided marketplace with an AI diagnostic layer

Repair Genie has two distinct interfaces — homeowner and contractor — connected by a shared job request and messaging system.

Homeowner flow

1
Creates a profile — name, address, phone — so contractors know how to reach them
2
Submits appliance details: type, brand, model, problem description, optional photo/video attachments
3
AI asks up to 2 clarifying questions, then produces a structured diagnosis: root causes with probability scores, safety checks, step-by-step DIY fix
4
If DIY fails or isn't safe, homeowner searches for contractors — filtered automatically by their address and the appliance type
5
Sends a job request — problem details only, no AI diagnosis (to prevent contractor bias)
6
Receives contractor responses via in-app messaging; can share photos and videos in thread
7
Contractor arrives — homeowner verifies identity using QR code match before letting them in
8
Contractor marks work complete; homeowner confirms; job closes with dual confirmation

Contractor flow

1
Creates profile: name, phone, bio, years of experience, service area, appliance specialties
2
Job requests arrive in inbox — filtered to only show jobs matching their specialty and service area
3
Reviews job: appliance, brand, model, problem description, attachments. No AI diagnosis shown — intentional design decision
4
Accepts, declines, or messages the homeowner to clarify before committing
5
On arrival, shows QR code to homeowner for identity verification
6
After completing repair, marks work done; job resolves when homeowner confirms

What's deliberately out of scope for v1

Payments Contractor ratings & reviews Real-time availability Push notifications Mobile app Calendar integration

The prototype facilitates the match, not the transaction. Ratings require job volume. Scope discipline kept v1 shippable in a hackathon timeframe.

Stage 04 · AI feature specification

The diagnosis engine

Model

Google Gemini 2.5 Flash — chosen for reasoning quality, speed, and generous free tier. Allows zero-cost prototype deployment.

Backend proxy

FastAPI on Render — all Gemini calls route server-side. The API key never touches the frontend. Output is sanitised before storage.

Confidence threshold

≥90% confidence before outputting a diagnosis. Below that, the model asks one clarifying question. Max 2 follow-ups before it must commit.

Token limit

8,192 output tokens — ensures responses are never truncated mid-diagnosis. Truncation was the most common failure mode in early testing.

Structured output schema

When the model is confident, it outputs a JSON block under the REPAIR_RESULT: marker. The backend parses this with brace-counting (not regex) for robustness. All string fields are HTML-stripped before storage.

{
  "causes": [{"label": "string", "probability": int}], // 1–4 causes, sum ≤ 100
  "safety": ["string"], // 2–5 safety checks
  "diy_possible": boolean, // false if gas/wiring/refrigerant
  "diy_steps": ["string"], // 3–8 plain-language steps
  "diy_note": "string", // explains if diy_possible = false
  "needs_contractor":boolean,
  "contractor_reason":"string"
}

Key design decisions

AI diagnosis hidden from contractors

Deliberately excluded from the job request. Contractors should diagnose independently — the AI is for the homeowner, not the repair industry. Prevents anchoring bias.

90% confidence threshold

Prevents the model from producing a diagnosis on insufficient information, which would erode user trust. Follow-up questions are the mechanism, not a fallback.

No streaming in v1

Single completion call per turn. Streaming would improve perceived latency — clear v2 improvement, but out of scope for a hackathon build.

Brace-counting JSON parser

Gemini occasionally wraps output in markdown code fences or adds trailing text. Brace-counting extracts the first valid JSON object reliably regardless of what surrounds it.

Stage 05 · Key features

What the product actually does

Smart contractor matching

When a homeowner searches for contractors, the system filters by two criteria: service area overlap (homeowner address checked against contractor's declared cities/towns) and appliance specialty (bidirectional substring match). A homeowner in Jersey City with a broken washer only sees contractors who cover Jersey City and list washer as a specialty.

Two-way in-app messaging

Homeowner and contractor communicate through a shared chat thread stored in a cross-tab JSON store. Either party can attach photos or videos — images render inline; other file types generate download links. Messages are visible to both parties regardless of which tab they're in.

QR-based identity verification

Before a contractor enters a homeowner's property, both parties verify identity using a QR code tied to the specific job. The QR encodes the job ID, contractor name, and appliance type. The homeowner's app shows the expected QR; the contractor's app displays the same. If they match — contractor name, job reference, and appliance all align — the homeowner can let the contractor in with confidence.

Why this matters

This feature addresses a real safety concern in the home services industry: homeowners letting strangers in based only on a phone call. The QR gives both parties cryptographic certainty tied to a specific job, not just a name.

Dual resolution confirmation

Issue resolution requires confirmation from both parties. Contractor marks work complete → homeowner is prompted to confirm → only when both confirm does the job close. If the homeowner declines, the job stays open and both parties are prompted to message. This creates an auditable resolution record and prevents contractors from closing jobs unilaterally.

Stage 06 · Success metrics

What we're measuring and why

Repair Genie is a two-sided marketplace. Success requires measuring both sides independently and the connection between them.

Metric Definition Target Why it matters
Diagnosis completion rate % of users who start a diagnosis and receive a REPAIR_RESULT >80% Core product promise: get an answer
DIY resolution rate % of diagnoses where homeowner marks resolved without contacting a contractor >35% Validates AI quality and user trust
Contractor match rate % of contractor searches that return ≥1 result >60% Validates network density
Job acceptance rate % of job requests accepted by a contractor >50% Validates job quality for contractors
Dual confirmation rate % of accepted jobs that reach full contractor + homeowner resolution >70% Validates end-to-end flow works
Contractor return rate % of contractors who accept a second job within 30 days >40% Validates contractor-side value

Anti-metrics — what we're deliberately not optimising for

Session length

Longer sessions are not better. Faster diagnosis is better.

Follow-up questions

Fewer is better, as long as diagnosis quality holds.

Contractor profiles created

Vanity metric without job acceptance to validate quality.

Stack

Key technical decisions

Shared file store

Cross-tab state is a JSON file with filelock for concurrency. Allows two browser tabs to share profiles, jobs, and messages — essential for a two-sided demo without a real database.

Media as base64

Chat attachments stored as base64 in the JSON store. Images render inline; other files get a download link. Works for demo scale; production needs object storage (S3/GCS).

Python 3.11 pin

Render defaulted to Python 3.14 which pydantic-core couldn't build on. Pinned to 3.11 via .python-version file — pre-built wheels, no Rust compilation needed.

FastAPI as AI proxy

All Gemini calls route through FastAPI. The API key never touches the Streamlit frontend. Backend also handles HTML sanitisation of all model outputs before they reach the UI.

What I'd do differently in production

Replace JSON store with Supabase

JSON file doesn't scale beyond a single server. PostgreSQL via Supabase gives multi-user, multi-server persistence with minimal setup.

WebSockets for messaging

Current app polls on page load. Production needs real-time push — WebSockets or server-sent events for instant message delivery.

Auth layer

Replace session-based identity with OAuth or magic link auth so users have persistent accounts across devices.

Stream AI responses

Single completion call per turn feels slow on complex diagnoses. Streaming would improve perceived latency significantly.

Stage 08 · What's next

v2 roadmap

Near term — 0 to 3 months

Payments via Stripe

Job deposits hold the contractor accountable and signal homeowner intent. Removes the biggest drop-off point between match and appointment.

Real contractor database

Replace JSON profiles with a real DB. Add contractor onboarding with background check integration and identity verification.

Push notifications

Alert contractors of new job requests and homeowners of responses in real time, not on next page load.

Mobile PWA

Streamlit is desktop-first. The on-site contractor experience demands a mobile-first interface — PWA or React Native.

Medium term — 3 to 9 months

Contractor ratings & reviews

Post-resolution, homeowner rates the contractor. Feeds into search ranking — creates the quality signal that makes the marketplace defensible.

Photo-based diagnosis

Pass actual image bytes to Gemini Vision, not just filenames. Allows visual diagnosis of burn marks, leaks, and error codes.

Parts sourcing

For DIY users, link directly to the replacement parts identified in the diagnosis. Affiliate revenue model, better UX for homeowners.

Appliance repair history

Homeowner builds a repair history per appliance. Genie uses prior sessions as context — a compounding advantage over generic AI search.

Long term — 9 to 18 months

Contractor CRM

Job history, earnings, customer notes — make Repair Genie the operating system for independent repair technicians, not just a lead source.

Property management B2B

Property managers as homeowner-side power users — manage repair workflows across multiple units. Natural upsell once consumer side is proven.
